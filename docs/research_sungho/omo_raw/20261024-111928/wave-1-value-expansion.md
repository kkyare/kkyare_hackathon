## 2. Yield loss from delayed weed control and missed fungicide timing

Weed control. The spray still happens; it is just late. That is a cleaner loss than fungicide, where skipping can save the chemical bill.

- University of Nebraska / Knezevic rule of thumb: about **2% yield loss per leaf stage** of delay past the critical time for weed removal, at moderate to high density. https://doi.org/10.1094/cm-2003-0219-01-rs and https://crops.extension.iastate.edu/encyclopedia/managing-weeds-protect-crop-yields
- **DERIVED** at the price anchors, assuming 180 bu corn and 55 bu soy: one stage is about **$15.30/acre corn** (3.6 bu × $4.25) and **$12.10/acre soy** (1.1 bu × $11).
- High-pressure corn, **INDEXED**: about **1.7 bu/acre per day** of POST delay, cited as ~$11/acre/day at $6.50/bu and ~$33 for three days. https://www.realagriculture.com/2024/05/delaying-post-emerge-weed-control-in-corn-can-be-costly/ At $4.25 that is **$7.23/acre/day**, or **$21.68 for a 3-day delay**.
- Soybeans, older price set: about **$3.50/acre/day** after the critical window in 30-inch rows. https://www.farmprogress.com/soybean/delayed-soybean-weed-control-costs-3-50-per-day
- A 5-day delay “up to 40 bu” in Minnesota trials is a high-pressure extreme, not a mean. https://www.farmprogress.com/crop-protection/late-weed-control-is-costly

Fungicide. At 2024 prices the average spray does not pay. Timing value is real, but price regime dominates.

farmdoc daily (14):83, 1 May 2024, fetched: https://farmdocdaily.illinois.edu/2024/05/fungicides-use-in-a-lower-price-environment.html

- Planning cost **$40/acre** product plus application; material alone **$25**.
- Break-even at $4.25 corn: **9.4 bu** to cover $40 (9.6 bu after harvest and haul). At $11 soy: **3.6 bu**.
- Paul et al. 2011 meta-analysis, 212 corn studies: average **+3.7 to +6.2 bu**, below that break-even. http://dx.doi.org/10.1094/PHYTO-03-11-0091
- Kandel et al. 2021, 240 soybean studies: average **+1.6 bu**. https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-07-20-1578-RE
- One extra moisture point at harvest was **$20–25/acre** of drying and shrink in their Ohio example. Not certain; stalk-quality papers sometimes offset it.
- **INDEXED** Corn Disease Working Group / Crop Protection Network: VT–R1 responses about 5–7.6 bu, vegetative about 1–3 bu, and above 11 bu when untreated severity exceeds 5%. https://cropprotectionnetwork.org/publications/impact-of-foliar-fungicide-timing-and-fungicide-class-on-corn-yield-response-in-the-united-states-and-ontario-canada

**DERIVED** fungicide arithmetic at $4.25: a 5.5 bu average response is worth $23 and costs $40, so skipping the average spray **saves about $17/acre**. An 11 bu high-disease hit is worth $47 against a $40 cost, so missing that window costs only about **$7/acre**. At the 2021-like $6.95 corn price in the same farmdoc figure, a 7 bu response is worth $49 against $40, and missing the window hurts. Do not sell fungicide timing as a large ROI at current row-crop prices. Sell it as “do not spray the average acre; do not miss the high-disease window.”

## 3. Nitrogen timing versus rain

The measured dollar result is a **weather-adjusted rate tool**, not a spray-window app.

Cornell / Adapt-N, 104 on-farm strip trials, New York and Iowa, 2011–2013. Fetched: https://blogs.cornell.edu/whatscroppingup/2014/10/02/adapt-n-boosts-profits-and-cuts-n-losses-in-three-years-of-on-farm-trials-in-new-york-and-iowa/

Most of that gain is **rate after rain has already fallen**, on growers who were already sidedressing, so Cornell calls it a conservative estimate versus all-preplant. A forecast that only says “do not put urea down ahead of an inch of rain” is a slice of this. I did not find a clean measured $/acre for that slice alone. Bayer’s qualitative line that extreme wet conditions can remove a large share of soil N is here: https://www.cropscience.bayer.us/articles/bayer/corn-management-loss-soil-nitrogen

Climate FieldView is the cautionary comp. ## Per-1,000-acre ROI table

Subscription cases: **$1,000 ($1/ac), $2,000 ($2/ac), $4,000 ($4/ac)**. ROI = expected benefit / subscription. Benefits are not additive across rows; the stack row is the one to use for a roadmap.

Incidence assumptions, all **ASSUMED** and the main sensitivity:

- Two herbicide spray-acres per acre per year (2,000 spray-acres).
- Conservative: 5% of spray-acres would be washed off; the tool prevents 60% of those.
- Base adds a weed-timing save on 15% of acres, one leaf stage, blended **$14/acre** (DERIVED from the 2% rule).
- Upside year: a weather system forces a respray on 40% of acres, and a 3-day high-pressure corn delay is avoided on 200 corn acres.

Reading the table:

- A normal-year herbicide window at **$2/acre is about a 2x** on the base case and **fails to clear 1x** on wash-off avoidance alone. At **$4/acre** (xarvio-like) the beachhead is a push in a normal year.
- The story that sells is row D, one bad spray week: about **8x at $2/acre**. That is not the mean. Cornell’s 77% hit rate on N is the right honesty standard: some years the tool saves nothing.
- Fungicide at $4.25 corn is mostly a **skip** decision (row E), not a “you left $30/acre in the field” decision (row F). Price regime can flip this.
- The expansion that is actually measured at tens of dollars per acre is **nitrogen rate after weather** (row G), and FieldView shows a bundled weather-plus-N tool can lose money. A spray-window MVP should not claim row G.

Price implication: **$1.00–1.50/acre, or a $649–1,500 farm license**, on 1,000 acres and up. That sits on top of free FieldView Basic and under FieldView Plus only if the decision is more specific than “here is the forecast” (label rainfast, inversion, wind, hours-to-rain). Do not price like Taranis. Add fungicide skip/hit and then N rate as paid modules once each has its own strip-trial number. Bill once a year, before planting, not monthly.

## EXPAND

1. Nitrogen rate module, not just sidedress timing. Cornell’s +$17 to +$37/acre is the only measured per-acre profit in this set that is an order of magnitude above a $2 tool. The product has to change the rate, incorporate manure and previous rain, and be honest that 23% of trials lost. FieldView’s negative Ohio results are the design constraint: show the strip, do not ship a black box.
2. Fungicide go/no-go tied to disease risk and the VT–R1 or R3 window, sold as avoided $40 sprays in low-price years. The dollar case is skipping, which is easier to prove on one season of records than a yield claim.
3. Residual activation and leaching, not only foliar rainfast. A 0.5–1 inch activation target versus a 4-inch leach risk is a different forecast product than “will it rain in four hours,” and it protects the expensive pass ($30 product), not the cheap glyphosate pass.
4. Custom-applicator dispatch for farms that do not own a second rig. The $9.35 is someone else’s margin. A tool that books the rig into a verified window is worth more to the operator and to the applicator than a grower-only app. That is the channel, and it also answers churn: the applicator renews if the window calls fill the week.
5. Inversion and drift liability is unpriced here. I did not find a clean $/acre for off-target dicamba or 2,4-D. If that number is even a few dollars of expected indemnity per acre, it dominates wash-off. Worth a separate pass on state indemnities and insurance claims before building the wind/inversion alert.
6. Do not expand into generic seasonal climate outlooks. The World Bank range of $0–128/acre is not a market. Farmers already get a forecast inside FieldView Plus for $0.65/acre. The wedge is the labeled operational decision.
